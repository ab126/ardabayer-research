Network Risk Estimation

Inferring cyber network risks in real-time

Overview

Network Risk Estimation (NRE) is a novel framework for estimating the risks of entities in a cyber network in real-time. By leveraging observed communications and third-party risk measurements, NRE provides dynamic risk assessments that adapt to incoming data, enhancing visibility of cybersecurity measures.

Below is a demonstration of the NRE. The demo simulates a small network, showcasing how NRE dynamically updates risk estimates as new batch of network flow data is processed.

Legend
  • Nodes: Network entities (e.g. users, servers, etc.)
  • Risk Scores: Color-coded from green (low risk) to red (high risk)
  • Red Edges: The inferred connectivity between entities
  • Yellow Edges: Flow data, packets were exchanged among entities for the flow batch
Urological Brain Computer Interface

Decoding supraspinal control of Lower Urinary Tract

Overview

Understanding the supraspinal control of the Lower Urinary Tract (LUT) is crucial for developing effective treatments for urinary disorders. This project focuses on decoding brain activity related to LUT function using advanced neuroimaging techniques and machine learning algorithms.

We employ functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) to capture brain activity during various LUT tasks. The collected data is then analyzed using manifold embedded functional connectivity methods to identify patterns and networks associated with LUT control. About

Developing principled computational methods to understand and control complex systems — from cyber networks to the human brain.

My research applies graph models, systems theory, and information theory to problems at the intersection of engineering and science. Previous work and current directions include:

  • Real-time risk estimation and inference in cyber-physical networks
  • Decoding supraspinal control of the lower urinary tract using fMRI, manifold learning and functional connectivity
  • Information-theoretic and graphical approaches to complex networked systems

I am currently a PhD student in the Aazhang Lab (Rice University) and a graduate fellow in the Khavari Lab (Houston Methodist), working at the interface of computational neuroscience, neuro-urology, and data science as of 2026.
